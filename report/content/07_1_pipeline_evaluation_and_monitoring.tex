This chapter provides a detailed overview of the pipeline evaluation and monitoring framework for the Lichess chess outcome prediction model.

\section{Model Performance Evaluation}
While developing this pipeline, I found that a multiclass classification model (predicting white win, black win, or draw) requires looking beyond simple accuracy due to problems such as class imbalances and the inherent difficulty of predicting draws. Our evaluation pipeline uses MLflow for experiment tracking and Evidently AI, Prometheus, Grafana for computing, tracking, and visualizing these performance metrics.

\section{Monitoring Strategy}
This pipeline also implements the monitoring and observability which covers three main pillars which are serving performance, data quality, and data drift.

Following indicators are being monitored in production:


\begin{itemize}
      \item Ratio of predicted classes for outcome.
        
      \item Sudden shifts in the distribution of predicted probabilities.

      \item Distributions of features features.

      \item Missing data rates.

      \item Classification metrics calculated on a daily basis.
  
      \item API operational health 

      \item System level resource usage like CPU and Memory usage.
    \end{itemize}

\section{Key Terms and Definitions}


\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data Protection Law}: Data Protection Law refers to the rules and regulations related to data protection and regulatory frameworks which are enacted by jurisdictions to govern the processing of personal data~\cite{european2016}. Examples include the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the European Union, the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in the United States, and the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) in Singapore~\cite{orcid}.

    \item \textbf{Data Privacy}: The fundamental right of individuals to control how their personal information is collected, processed, stored, and shared~\cite{westin1967}. It focuses on user autonomy, transparency, consent, and individual rights over personal space~\cite{orcid}.

\end{itemize}


\subsection{General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)}
The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) establishes the Right to Erasure, commonly known as the Right to be Forgotten (RTBF), under Article 17~\cite{european2016}. This article grants individuals the right to have their personal data deleted by a data controller without undue delay under specific circumstances, such as when the data is no longer necessary for its original purpose or when the user withdraws consent~\cite{gdpr_forgotten}. Since, the data we used contains the username of the player, how we handle the username remains the crucial task for complying with the GDPR regulation.
