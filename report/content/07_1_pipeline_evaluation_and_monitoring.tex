\chapter{Pipeline Evaluation and Monitoring}
\label{ch:evaluation-and-monitoring}
This chapter provides a detailed overview of the pipeline evaluation and monitoring framework for the Lichess chess outcome prediction model.

\section{Model Performance Evaluation}
While developing this pipeline, I found that a multiclass classification model (predicting white win, black win, or draw) requires looking beyond simple accuracy due to problems such as class imbalances and the inherent difficulty of predicting draws. Our evaluation pipeline uses MLflow for experiment tracking and Evidently AI, Prometheus, Grafana for computing, tracking, and visualizing these performance metrics.

\subsection{Classification Metrics}
Evidently AI calculates following classification metrics as shown in Figure \ref{fig:classification_metrics}, \ref{fig:confusion_matrix_1} and \ref{fig:confusion_matrix_2}  on our validation and test splits, including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score for each class (White Win, Black Win, Draw). During the implementation, I observed the following patterns in the metrics:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy vs. F1-score:} Accuracy alone can be overly optimistic due to class distribution (draws are less common than wins). Monitoring macro-averaged F1-scores provides a more robust measure of performance across all outcomes.
    \item \textbf{Class Breakdown:} Performance is typically higher on decisive outcomes (wins/losses) compared to draws, reflecting the complexity of predicting peaceful results from pre-game features.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Confusion Matrix Interpretation}
The confusion matrix helps identify systematic misclassifications by mapping true outcomes against predicted outcomes. The model successfully separates white wins from black wins with high reliability, demonstrating strong predictive capability for decisive outcomes. In contrast, it often misclassifies draws as wins for either side because draw boundaries are highly non-linear and are strongly influenced by micro-tactical developments that cannot be fully captured using pre-game features such as ratings and time controls. Further details are presented in Figures \ref{fig:confusion_matrix_1} and \ref{fig:confusion_matrix_2}, which show the distribution of true vs. predicted classes and highlight areas where the model struggles, particularly in distinguishing draws from wins.

\section{Monitoring Strategy}
Our production observability strategy covers three main pillars: serving performance, data quality, and concept/data drift.

\subsection{What is being monitored:}
Following indicators are being monitored in production:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Predictions:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The ratio of predicted classes (White Win, Black Win, Draw) is monitored continuously.
        \item Sudden shifts in the distribution of predicted probabilities are tracked.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Features:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Distributions of numerical features (player/opponent Elo, Elo difference) and categorical features (ECO opening codes, game types).
        \item Missing data rates (null values) are tracked to ensure upstream ingestion/scraping processes remain healthy.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Performance \& Infrastructure:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Live classification metrics (accuracy, precision, recall) are calculated on a daily lag by matching prediction logs in MariaDB ColumnStore against true game outcomes.
        \item API operational health is tracked via request throughput (QPS), HTTP latency, memory usage, CPU load, and network I/O.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Retraining policy}
We manage pipeline orchestration, drift analysis, and model retraining using \textbf{Apache Airflow}. Since, the data size is exponentially increasing on lichess platform we have set up a notification alert system  to slack channel which informs the team with reasong for retraining. Our monitoring pipeline runs daily. Retrain automatically happens at first day of each month reason being lichess publishing the data on lichess portal each month.

\section{Ethical, Legal, Security and Privacy Considerations}
Operating an analytical pipeline on public Lichess data requires aligning its architecture with Lichess's legal and operational standards~\cite{lichess_privacy}. Lichess is a libre, open‑source platform that processes data based on its legitimate interest in developing and improving its services, in accordance with Article 6.1.f of the GDPR~\cite{lichess_privacy}. Lichess applies European data protection standards to all users globally, regardless of their nationality~\cite{lichess_privacy}. To support open science and academic research, Lichess releases its game and puzzle databases under the Creative Commons CC0 Public Domain Dedication and the broadcast games are released under CC BY‑SA 4.0~\cite{lichess_license}. This open‑data philosophy has supported over 200 academic papers, including research by Google DeepMind to train chess models and composition systems~\cite{reddit2025}. However, while a CC0 waiver relinquishes copyright and database rights, it cannot waive data subjects' privacy rights under the GDPR~\cite{edpb2023}.

\subsubsection{Key Definitions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data Privacy}: The fundamental right of individuals to control how their personal information is collected, processed, stored, and shared~\cite{westin1967}. It focuses on user autonomy, transparency, consent, and individual rights over personal space~\cite{orcid}.

    \item \textbf{Information Security}: The technical, administrative, and physical practices designed to protect data from unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration, or destruction~\cite{iso27001}. It is governed by the core principles of confidentiality, integrity, and availability (the CIA triad)~\cite{orcid}. While data privacy defines who has authorised access, information security provides the mechanisms to enforce those boundaries~\cite{orcid}.

    \item \textbf{Data Ethics}: The moral and normative frameworks that guide the responsible design, development, and use of technology~\cite{floridi2016}. Ethical governance ensures that systems are fair, transparent, and accountable, preventing algorithmic bias, discrimination, or exploitation of vulnerable populations~\cite{floridi2016}.

    \item \textbf{Data Protection Law}: The binding statutory and regulatory frameworks enacted by jurisdictions to govern the processing of personal data (e.g., GDPR in the EU, CCPA in California, DPDP Act in India)~\cite{european2016}. These laws establish legal obligations for data controllers and processors while providing enforceable rights to data subjects~\cite{european2016}.

    \item \textbf{Data Provenance}: The documentation of the history of a data object, structured into source provenance (tracking the origins of data), transformation provenance (documenting changes and processes), and usage provenance (detailing access and consumption)~\cite{iso27001}.
\end{itemize}

By integrating statistical drift detection with privacy‑preserving architectures, developers can build resilient, compliant systems~\cite{mdpi2025}. Implementing the ADWIN algorithm enables early identification of model decay, while incorporating explainability tools like SHAP helps developers understand the underlying causes of drift~\cite{baena2006}. On the privacy front, combining salted cryptographic pseudonymisation, metadata generalisation, and SISA retraining shards allows pipelines to satisfy GDPR Article 17 requirements without sacrificing the scientific value of open chess data~\cite{edpb2023}. Ultimately, treating data privacy, information security, and model monitoring as integrated requirements ensures that downstream analytics remain accurate, secure, and legally compliant~\cite{floridi2016}.


\subsection{General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)}
The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) establishes the \textit{Right to Erasure}, commonly known as the Right to be Forgotten (RTBF), under Article 17~\cite{european2016}. This article grants individuals the right to have their personal data deleted by a data controller without undue delay under specific circumstances, such as when the data is no longer necessary for its original purpose or when the user withdraws consent~\cite{gdpr_forgotten}. The territorial scope of this right, clarified by the Court of Justice of the European Union in \textit{Google v. CNIL} (2019), applies within the borders of the EU Member States, rather than globally, balancing individual privacy with global access to information~\cite{google_cnil}. For removing data from the scope of the GDPR entirely, organisations often apply anonymisation techniques~\cite{edpb2023}. Recital 26 of the GDPR distinguishes between \textit{pseudonymised data} which can still be re‑attributed to an individual using auxiliary information and therefore remains within the scope of the law and anonymised data, which is irreversibly altered so that re‑identification is impossible~\cite{edpb2023}.

\subsection{Relevance to Lichess pipeline}
When a user deletes their Lichess account, the platform anonymises the account~\cite{lichess_deletion}. Direct identifiers (such as emails, IP logs, and OAuth tokens) are erased, but public game records are retained to preserve the integrity of the public archive and historical game statistics~\cite{lichess_deletion}. The usernames in these games are replaced with "Anonymous" or decoupled from the deleted profile~\cite{lichess_deletion}. For a downstream MLOps pipeline ingesting these game dumps, this architecture introduces several governance challenges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PGN‑based re‑identification}: A player's move sequence serves as a unique behavioural signature~\cite{pychess}. By linking anonymised game records from monthly Lichess dumps with external sources—such as public streaming profiles, forum posts, or tournament sheets—adversaries can de‑anonymise "Anonymous" accounts, exposing sensitive metadata like playing times or geolocation data~\cite{lichess4545}.
    \item \textbf{Data synchronisation failures}: While Lichess processes deletion requests, third‑party pipelines often run on older, cached database dumps that still contain the unscrubbed usernames~\cite{lichess4545}. If these models continue to use outdated datasets, the pipeline operator may face direct legal liability under GDPR Article 17~\cite{lichess4545}.
    \item \textbf{Automated profiling constraints}: Downstream systems that analyse gameplay patterns to suggest improvements or detect anomalies must ensure their automated processing does not produce significant legal effects on users, or provide manual appeal paths, as observed in platforms like EloShift~\cite{eloshift}.
\end{itemize}
